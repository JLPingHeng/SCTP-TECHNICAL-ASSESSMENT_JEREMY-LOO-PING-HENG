# Question 6 — AI Literacy: Prompt Engineering

**Format:** Short Answer

---

Read both prompts below carefully, then answer the questions that follow.

**Prompt A**

> I am a marketing manager at a retail company and we have just finished a three-month campaign. My team has collected customer feedback through an online survey and we now have about 500 responses stored in a spreadsheet. Each response includes the customer's age group, the product they purchased, their satisfaction rating from 1 to 5, and a short written comment. I need to present the findings to our CEO next Friday in a way that is easy to understand. Can you analyse this data for me, highlight which age groups and products have the lowest satisfaction scores, identify the most common complaints from the written comments, and summarise everything in a short paragraph I can use as an executive summary?

**Prompt B**

> **Role:** You are a data analyst helping a retail marketing team.  
> **Task:** Analyse customer survey data from a 3-month campaign.  
> **Data:** 500 responses containing age group, product purchased, satisfaction rating (1-5), and written comments.  
> **Steps:**
> 1. Identify age groups and products with the lowest satisfaction scores.
> 2. Extract the most common themes from the written comments.
> 3. Summarise findings in an executive summary paragraph.
>
> **Audience:** CEO presentation on Friday.  
> **Constraints:** Keep the summary concise and free of technical jargon.

---

## Task 1 — Analysis

**(a)** Which prompt do you think will get a better response from an AI? State your choice (A or B).

**Answer:**  
_Prompt B_

---

**(b)** Give **two reasons** to support your choice.

**Reason 1:**  
_Prompt B breaks the request down into distinct, easily parseable fields (Role, Task, Data, Steps, Audience, Constraints). Generative AI models parse structured text (i.e. Prompt B) much more reliably than dense blocks of paragraphs (i.e. Prompt A). Isolating the specific Steps ensures that GenAI executes every part of the required analysis without skipping secondary requests buried in a long paragraph._

**Reason 2:**  
_Prompt B includes a Constraints: section ("Keep the summary concise and free of technical jargon") alongside a clear Audience: parameter ("CEO presentation"). Explicitly defining constraints and audience parameters in Prompt B forces GenAI model to adjust its tone, depth, and formatting directly for C-suite executive readiness, preventing overly technical jargon or unnecessary fluff. Whereas in Prompt A, the target audience and presentation goals are blended into narrative text._

---

**(c)** What is **one strength** of the prompt you did NOT choose?

**Answer:**  
_The primary strength of Prompt A is its natural, contextual narrative. Prompt A is written in a continuous, conversational story. It provides richer background context—such as how the data was collected and stored (i.e. an online survey stored in a spreadsheet) and the real-world timeline/pressure behind the task. For quick, informal interactions, this conversational style is faster and more intuitive to type out without having to pause and structure custom formatting templates._

## Task 2 — Rewrite

Rewrite either prompt by borrowing **one element** from the other to make it stronger. Then explain what you borrowed and why.

**Your rewritten prompt:**
> **Prompt B**
> 
> **Role:** You are a data analyst helping a retail marketing team.  
> **Task:** Analyse customer survey data from a 3-month campaign.  
> **Data:** 500 responses in a spreadsheet, where each entry contains:
> - Customer's age group
> - Product purchased
> - Satisfaction rating (Scale from 1 to 5)
> - A short written comment
> 
> **Steps:**
> 1. Identify age groups and products with the lowest satisfaction scores.
> 2. Extract the most common themes from the written comments.
> 3. Summarise findings in an executive summary paragraph.
>
> **Audience:** CEO presentation on Friday.  
> **Constraints:** Keep the summary concise, actionable and free of technical jargon.


**What you borrowed and why:**

> **What was borrowed:** The itemized details of the dataset columns from Prompt A ("Each response includes the customer's age group, the product they purchased, their satisfaction rating from 1 to 5, and a short written comment"). 
> 
> **Why it makes the prompt stronger:** In the original Prompt B, the "Data" line was a brief summary. Explicitly listing the specific data variables gives GenAI exact structural clarity on the available information to analyze. This prevents GenAI from assuming missing variables or generating generic responses, resulting in a much more accurate and targeted analysis.
